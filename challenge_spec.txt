QUANTUM TRAFFIC GRIDLOCK
 
Quantum Optimization for Coordinated Urban Traffic Signals | IBM Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 | QC@IITI · IIT Indore
1. BACKGROUND
Traffic intersections influence one another: allowing vehicles through one junction can increase queues downstream.
Develop a quantum optimization workflow that coordinates traffic-light phases across a simplified network. The emphasis is
on mathematical modelling, QUBO construction, QAOA implementation, and honest experimental validation—not building a
realistic traffic simulator.
2. CHALLENGE OBJECTIVE
Build a scenario-independent program that accepts a traffic scenario, constructs a justified QUBO (or Ising Hamiltonian),
runs QAOA, and returns a signal schedule and its raw sampled distribution. Hidden scenarios will test generalization. No
QUBO coefficients, Hamiltonian, penalty weights, or optimization solution will be supplied.
5. OFFICIAL COST & SCORER
Minimize cost = delay + overflow_weight × overflow + switch_weight × switches. Delay sums all remaining queues after
service across slots (vehicle-slots); overflow sums excess above nominal storage; switches count changes between
adjacent slots at each intersection (no pre-slot-1 switch cost). The released scorer.py is the authoritative evaluator. It takes
scenario JSON and an eight-bit schedule, but does not construct QUBO or optimize.
3. MANDATORY TRAFFIC MODEL
Two connected intersections, A and B, over four 30-second slots. Each intersection gives green to exactly one phase per slot: 
1 = North–South (NS), 0 = East–West (EW). The eight decision bits are ordered A slots 1–4 followed by B slots 1–4. A quadratic surrogate
is allowed; auxiliary variables may increase the QUBO size beyond eight.
For each slot: (1) add external arrivals and transfers from the preceding slot; (2) the green approach serves up to its capacity;
(3) record delay and overflow from queues remaining after service; (4) on the directed A-to-B link, transfer floor(fraction ×
departures + 0.5) vehicles from the linked approach into B at the beginning of the next slot. Transfers after the last slot leave
the model. Other departures leave the model. Queues start empty. Overflow is penalized, not forbidden; all 256 schedules
are valid.

Hidden scenarios retain the same traffic model and eight decisions, varying arrivals (0–8), capacity (4–6), storage (5–8), overflow
weight (2–6), switch weight (0.25–1.5), and a single A →  B link on NS or EW with fraction 0.4–0.8. Teams must not hard-code R. Hour
0: PS, scenario R and scorer released. Hour 18: shared evaluation scenario H released. Hour 24: tagged repository, report and outputs
for R and H submitted. After the event: judges run the same tagged code on unseen scenario S.
Limits: at most 16 qubits; QAOA depth p ≤  4; at most 300 training objective evaluations; exactly 4,096 final shots; deterministic seed;
CPU-only and no network during judging; at most 10 minutes per seed on an organizer-specified 8 GB reference environment. Fewer
shots may be used during training. Classical refinement is allowed only as a separately labelled hybrid result, not as raw-QAOA
sampling output. Judges may check the QUBO, circuit, and counts for consistency.
For each scenario, judges enumerate all 256 schedules to compute the best official cost C* and uniform-random mean C_rand. E_Q
is the mean official cost of the 4,096 raw QAOA samples. Lift = (C_rand − E_Q)/(C_rand − C*). Organizers exclude hidden scenarios
where the denominator is too small.
Elite enrichment: select exactly the 13 lowest-cost schedules, breaking ties by canonical ascending bitstring. Enrichment = (fraction of
shots in that elite set)/(13/256); random sampling has expected enrichment 1. Robustness: report lift across at least five fixed seeds;
judges use the minimum lift clipped to [0,1], avoiding unstable ratios. Final thresholds and seeds will be announced before the
challenge begins. Finding a single optimum among 4,096 shots is not sufficient evidence of QAOA performance.

Submit a public GitHub repository containing the runnable pipeline, instructions, and outputs for R and H; a technical report of at

13. EVALUATION RUBRIC (100 marks)
Judging is entirely submission-based. Organizers will inspect the tagged code, rerun the pipeline, compare the official scores, and
evaluate the written report. Incorrect or non-reproducible quantum results cannot earn full quantum-performance credit.
Performance thresholds and judging hardware must be frozen and published before the event.
